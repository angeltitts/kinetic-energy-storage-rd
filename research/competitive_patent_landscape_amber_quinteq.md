# Competitive & Patent Landscape — Amber Kinetics and QuinteQ/Boeing

## Executive finding

The current Gen-6 concept overlaps materially with an active Boeing patent family licensed exclusively worldwide to QuinteQ.

US8922081B2 (Nested-rotor open-core flywheel) is active in the US and currently listed with an adjusted expiration of 2033-05-03. Its independent claim 1 covers a vacuum-chamber flywheel with multiple different-radius rotors, rotor/stator magnets, and an HTS bearing arranged to levitate the rotor. Dependent claims add open-core architecture, CNT materials, and counter-rotation.

This does not by itself prove infringement of any future product; infringement is determined by the issued claims and jurisdiction-specific law. But it is a major freedom-to-operate red flag for Gen-6 as presently conceived.

## QuinteQ / Boeing

QuinteQ states that it secured a worldwide exclusive license and technology transfer from Boeing covering more than 200 patents. Its commercial THOR product is a short-duration, high-power flywheel:
- 300 kW
- 4 kWh
- 40 ms response
- 6 tonne system
- >250,000 cycles on current product page
- >15 year service life

QuinteQ also reports 600 kW building blocks for THOR X, scalable to 4.2 MW, and describes a Boeing-derived carbon-fiber rotor spinning around 22,000 rpm.

Key Boeing patent families that are directly relevant:
- US8922081B2 — nested-rotor open-core flywheel — ACTIVE, US expiration listed 2033-05-03.
- US9729025B2 / foreign counterparts — open-core flywheel architecture.
- US8698365B2 / EP2647874B1 — lightweight composite safety containment.
- EP2647875B1 — flexible magnet directional stiffening.
- older Boeing HTS flywheel-bearing patents, some expired and some incorporated into later active families.

## Amber Kinetics

Amber took the opposite engineering path: lower-speed, heavy steel rotor optimized for cost, durability, long duration, and low self-discharge.

Current M32 public data:
- 32 kWh
- 8 kW
- >=4 h discharge
- >86% DC round-trip efficiency
- <100 W average self-discharge
- 10,500 lb system
- 30-year design life in installation manual/brochure
- hybrid mechanical + magnetic bearing arrangement
- historical commercial-scale test through ~8,500 rpm
- 10-50 mTorr vacuum in DOE testing

Important Amber active patent families include:
- US9362800B2 — preloaded wire-wound steel rotor
- US10003237B2 — solid steel flywheel rotor/process
- US10138980B2 and later continuations — stacked flywheel rotor
- US9892839B2 — magnetic unloading of rotor bearing
- US10693342B2 — field containment / site arrangement
- US10998803B2 / EP3479464B1 — homopolar motor
- US11682943B2 — motor/generator with atmospheric stator pressure
- US11271452B2 — bifurcated molecular pump
- later stacked-lamination rotor/endplate patents through 2025

## Key lesson

Commercial flywheel systems are succeeding with extremely low gravimetric system energy density compared with batteries:
- Amber M32: ~32 kWh / 4,763 kg ≈ 6.7 Wh/kg system
- QuinteQ THOR: 4 kWh / 6,000 kg ≈ 0.67 Wh/kg system

Therefore the project's earlier 500 Wh/kg complete-system target is not required for a viable stationary product. For stationary industrial markets, the higher-value metrics are:
- $/kW
- power density
- cycle life
- response time
- self-discharge
- maintenance interval
- safety / fire risk
- installation cost
- ability to avoid grid upgrades
