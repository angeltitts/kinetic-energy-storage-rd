# CNT Fiber Benchmark — Dynamic and Quasi-Static Strength (Science, 2024)

Source: Zhang, X. et al., "Carbon nanotube fibers with dynamic strength up to 14 GPa," *Science* 384, 1318-1323 (2024).

DOI: 10.1126/science.adj1082

## Source-supported facts

- The work reports a macroscale carbon-nanotube fiber produced through hierarchical structural optimization, including improved interfacial interaction, alignment, and densification.
- Reported quasi-static tensile strength: **8.2 GPa**.
- Reported dynamic tensile strength: **14 GPa** at a strain rate of approximately **1400 s^-1**.
- The authors attribute the high-rate performance to more synchronous CNT breakage and impact-energy delocalization as interfacial interactions, alignment, and densification improve.
- The reported specific energy dissipation power under the transverse-impact experiment was **(8.7 ± 1.0) × 10^13 m·kg^-1·s^-1**.

## Relevance to C-002

This is direct evidence that macroscale CNT fibers can sustain multi-GPa longitudinal tensile stresses. It does **not** establish the register's required 20 GPa long-life usable rotor stress, nor does a high-strain-rate dynamic strength substitute for fatigue/creep-qualified quasi-static design strength.

The accessible source material used for this benchmark does not provide a sufficiently verified density value for converting the 8.2 GPa quasi-static result into a project-grade specific-strength comparison, so no density-derived number is inserted here.

## Evidence classification

- Source type: peer-reviewed journal article plus institutional summary
- Scale: macroscale CNT fiber
- Loading: quasi-static and high-strain-rate tensile
- Direct relevance: longitudinal strength, dynamic failure mechanism
- Missing for rotor qualification: density verified for the tested fiber, long-duration creep, fatigue S-N data, environmental stability, manufacturable rotor-scale continuity
