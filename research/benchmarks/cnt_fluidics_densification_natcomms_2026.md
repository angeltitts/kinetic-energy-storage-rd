# CNT Fiber Benchmark — Fluidics-Induced Alignment and Densification (Nature Communications, 2026)

Source: Wang, J. N. et al., "Improvement of the tensile strength of carbon nanotube fibers to 12.5 GPa by fluidics-induced alignment and densification," *Nature Communications* 17, 8231 (2026).

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-75150-1

## Source-supported facts

- Reported absolute tensile strength: **12.5 GPa**.
- Reported specific tensile strength: **7.5 × 10^6 N·m/kg** (7.5 N/tex).
- Reported Young's modulus: **370 GPa**.
- Reported electrical conductivity: approximately **3 × 10^6 S/m**.
- Reported thermal conductivity: approximately **968 W/m/K**.
- Measured inter-CNT shear strength: **0.035 MPa** for aligned strips and **0.005 MPa** for unaligned strips.
- The paper links improved strength to CNT alignment, dense packing, long CNT length, and increased inter-tube load transfer.

## Direct comparison with the register baseline

The C-002 register baseline is 20 GPa at 1,500 kg/m^3:

    specific strength = 20e9 / 1500 = 1.33e7 N·m/kg

The paper's reported best specific tensile strength is:

    7.5e6 N·m/kg

Therefore the demonstrated result is only about **56% of the register baseline specific strength**. This is meaningful progress for macroscale CNT fibers but is negative-leaning evidence for the current 20 GPa / 1,500 kg/m^3 requirement, not confirmation.

## Relevance to D-002

The measured inter-CNT shear strengths (0.035/0.005 MPa) are orders of magnitude below the 12.5 GPa longitudinal tensile strength. That extreme anisotropy supports avoiding architectures that require substantial radial/inter-layer shear transfer to exploit longitudinal CNT strength.

## Evidence classification

- Source type: peer-reviewed, open-access journal article
- Scale: macroscale CNT fiber / thin ribbon specimens
- Loading: tensile and interfacial shear measurements
- Direct relevance: specific strength and anisotropic load transfer
- Missing for rotor qualification: fatigue S-N behavior at high mean stress, creep under long-duration charged hold, rotor-scale manufacturability, defect statistics at large circumference
