# Decision Proposal — Freeze Gen-6 Commercialization, Open Patent-Conscious Track

## Recommended decision

1. Keep Gen-6 as a research concept only.
2. Do not spend prototype money on an HTS + nested independent rotor implementation intended for commercialization until a patent attorney reviews US8922081B2 and its foreign family.
3. Start a parallel clean-sheet product architecture optimized for stationary peak-power applications, not 500 Wh/kg.
4. Set product success metrics around $/kW, cycles, response, safety, maintenance, and grid-upgrade avoidance.
5. Use a single rotor and non-cryogenic bearing system as the default design-around starting point.

## Why now

The strongest new evidence is that the project's most distinctive architecture is not white space: Boeing patented essentially the same nested independent rotor + HTS open-core direction years ago, and QuinteQ says it holds the exclusive worldwide license.

At the same time, both Amber and QuinteQ prove that stationary flywheel products can be commercially useful at system energy densities orders of magnitude below 500 Wh/kg.

This changes both the IP strategy and the product objective.

## Kill / proceed gates

Proceed with clean-sheet product only if:
- a preliminary claim chart finds a credible design-around path;
- a 50-150 kW / 0.5-2 kWh product has a believable TCO advantage in one high-cycle use case;
- modeled standby loss, bearing life, and containment cost are credible.

Kill or license if:
- the practical architecture still reads on active core claims;
- required containment and bearing costs eliminate TCO advantage;
- customer economics do not beat batteries + grid upgrade alternatives.
