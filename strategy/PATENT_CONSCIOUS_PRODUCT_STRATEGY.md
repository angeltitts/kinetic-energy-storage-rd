# Product Strategy — Patent-Conscious Flywheel Track

## Recommended pivot

Do not use the present Gen-6 nested, different-radius, independently rotating, HTS-bearing architecture as the default commercial path.

Reason: it is unusually close to the active Boeing/QuinteQ nested-rotor open-core patent family.

Instead, create a separate commercial design track that deliberately avoids the core combination of:
- multiple nested different-radius rotors
- HTS levitation
- open-core architecture
- rotor/stator magnet layout of the Boeing family

Also avoid Amber-specific implementations such as:
- preloaded wire-wound steel rotor
- Amber stacked-lamination rotor/endplate geometry
- Amber homopolar M/G
- atmospheric-stator vacuum barrier
- bifurcated molecular pump
- Amber field-containment geometry

## Proposed commercial product target

A single-rotor, non-cryogenic, short-duration industrial peak-power module.

Target use:
- cranes and hoists
- elevators
- port equipment
- EV fast-charging sites with weak grid connections
- industrial welding/press/pulse loads
- battery-life-extension in hybrid storage
- microgrid transient support

Initial product envelope to investigate:
- 50-150 kW
- 0.5-2 kWh
- 20-120 s useful full-power duration depending configuration
- DC-bus integration
- response <100 ms
- >100,000 deep cycles
- above-ground serviceable enclosure
- no cryogenic system

## Why this is commercially more plausible

QuinteQ demonstrates that customers will buy a 300 kW / 4 kWh system weighing ~6 tonnes because the value is peak shaving, cycling, and avoiding grid upgrades.

Amber demonstrates the other extreme: customers will buy a 32 kWh / 8 kW, ~4.8 tonne system because long duration, low self-discharge, long life, and safety can matter more than Wh/kg.

The market gap worth testing is a lower-cost, non-cryogenic, modular high-cycle power buffer between these extremes.

## Technical design-around hypothesis

Preferred architecture for the clean-sheet track:
- one rotor per vacuum vessel
- conventional shaft/hub geometry or another pre-2012 public-domain architecture
- active magnetic bearing OR hybrid magnetic/mechanical bearing, not HTS
- standard PMSM or switched-reluctance M/G, not Amber homopolar topology
- conventional external vacuum pump/getter architecture, not Amber bifurcated pump
- conventional containment developed from public-domain principles with an independent claim review
- modular cabinets connected on a common DC bus

This is NOT a legal freedom-to-operate conclusion. It is a design-around starting point.

## Required legal gate before commercialization

Commission a patent attorney to perform a formal FTO search and claim chart in each launch jurisdiction, at minimum:
- US
- EU
- UK
- Canada
- Australia
- target Asian markets

The FTO should include Boeing/QuinteQ, Amber Kinetics, Beacon, Torus, Revterra, Temporal Power lineage, Raytheon, and major magnetic-bearing / composite-rotor families.
